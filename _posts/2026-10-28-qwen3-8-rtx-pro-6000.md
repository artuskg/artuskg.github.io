*28 October 2026. Benchmark results from a five-day optimization campaign, not a peer-reviewed paper.*

Three setups, one goal: finish long-context agentic coding tasks sooner at the same quality. We tuned kernels, 4-bit quantization and speculative decoding for Qwen3.8 27B on one GPU and for Qwen3.8-Flash-Next on one or two GPUs.

Everything was measured from 24 to 28 October 2026 on NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition GPUs (AWS g7e Spot), with a single stream unless stated. The glossary at the end explains the short names. Hover over a chart for exact values, or open its data table. The repetition loops counted in the GPU-time chart are the subject of the [previous post](/2026/10/27/when-an-agent-repeats-its-plan.html).

<section class="card" id="c-method">
 <div class="kicker">Method</div>
 <h2 id="method">How these numbers were measured</h2>
 <div class="method">
  <p><b>Hardware.</b> NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition (96 GB, SM120), on AWS g7e Spot instances: one GPU for the 27B and Flash Next 1-GPU setups, two GPUs with tensor parallelism for Flash Next. Speed was measured only on this GPU; the 32 GB RTX PRO 4500 was used for fit and quality checks only. No other heavy work ran during speed runs.</p>
  <p><b>Serving.</b> vLLM nightly with our patches. Decode speed: one stream, greedy, thinking on, 2,048 output tokens after real code-review prompts of 76.7K, 126.1K and 223.7K tokens, three prompts per length, pooled as (tokens − 1) ÷ decode time. Fresh engine start for each benchmark.</p>
  <p><b>Quality gates.</b> Every adopted stack keeps GSM8K (1,319 problems; 200 for early 27B rows) within its bar, passes 18 of 18 needle-retrieval probes at 64K–200K, and meets the W01 grade bar with no repetition loops. Speculative decoding with greedy verification is lossless; 4-bit weights, the 4-bit KV cache and sparse prefill change the numerics and are judged by these gates.</p>
  <p><b>Caveats.</b> Ratios against the stock setups are across hosts unless stated. W01 has six frozen tasks, so repeated runs are repeat episodes of the same tasks; trajectories differ between runs, and run-to-run spread is about ±5%.</p>
